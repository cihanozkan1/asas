## 2. The Darién Gap: The Road That Stops in the Jungle 🌴🇵🇦🇨🇴😱  (`darien_gap`)
- The Pan-American Highway is about 30,000 km, from Prudhoe Bay, Alaska, to Ushuaia, Argentina. — [Wikipedia: Pan-American_Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-American_Highway)
  > "from Prudhoe Bay, Alaska, United States, in the northernmost part of North America, to Ushuaia, Argentina"
- The highway links 14 countries. — [Wikipedia: Pan-American_Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-American_Highway)
  > "The highway connects 14 countries: Canada, the United States, Mexico, Guatemala, El Salvador, Honduras, Nicaragua, Costa Rica, Panama, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, Chile, and Argentina."
- The highway breaks at Yaviza, Panama and resumes at Turbo, Colombia, roughly 106 km away. — [Wikipedia: Darién_Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darién_Gap)
  > "The 'Gap' interrupts the Pan-American Highway, which breaks at Yaviza, Panama, and resumes at Turbo, Colombia, roughly 106 km (66 mi) away."
- Vehicles must be shipped by cargo vessel to get around the gap. — [Wikipedia: Pan-American_Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-American_Highway)
  > "vehicles must be shipped by cargo vessel to bridge this section"
- Colombian side: Atrato delta marshland; Panamanian side: mountainous rainforest. — [Wikipedia: Darién_Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darién_Gap)
  > "the Colombian side dominated primarily by the river delta of the Atrato River, which creates a flat marshland at least 80 km (50 mi) wide"
- Rain in the Darién Gap produces flash floods. — [Wikipedia: Darién_Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darién_Gap)
  > "Rainfall in the Darién Gap produces flash floods that can carry sleepers to their deaths."
- Road planning began in 1971 with US funding and was halted in 1974 after environmentalists raised concerns. — [Wikipedia: Darién_Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darién_Gap)
  > "Planning began in 1971 with the help of US funding, but was halted in 1974 after multiple environmentalists expressed serious concerns."
- In 1978 the US Department of Agriculture blocked US support to prevent the spread of foot-and-mouth disease. — [Wikipedia: Darién_Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darién_Gap)
  > "US support was further blocked by the US Department of Agriculture in 1978, with the intention of preventing the spread of foot-and-mouth disease."
- In 1994 a UN agency reported that the road would cause extensive environmental damage. — [Wikipedia: Darién_Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darién_Gap)
  > "but by 1994, a United Nations agency reported that the road and the subsequent development would cause extensive environmental damage."
- Five tribes comprising 8,000 people have said a road could erode their cultures. — [Wikipedia: Darién_Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darién_Gap)
  > "The Embera-Wounaan and Guna are among five tribes, comprising 8,000 people, who have expressed concern that the road would potentially result in erosion of their cultures."
- Dangers include venomous wildlife and tropical diseases. — [Wikipedia: Darién_Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darién_Gap)
  > "venomous and deadly wildlife, tropical insects, parasites and diseases, and frequent heavy rains and flash floods"
- Law enforcement and medical support are nonexistent; violent crime is rampant. — [Wikipedia: Darién_Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darién_Gap)
  > "law enforcement and medical support are nonexistent, resulting in rampant violent crime"
- Crossings: more than 130,000 in 2021, about 250,000 in 2022, more than 520,000 in 2023. — [Wikipedia: Darién_Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darién_Gap)
  > "In 2023, more than 520,000 individuals passed through the gap"
- The 2021 and 2022 crossings. — [Wikipedia: Darién_Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darién_Gap)
  > "more than 130,000"

## 8. The Invisible Highways Planes Use Over the Atlantic ✈️🌊🤯  (`atlantic_tracks`)
- The North Atlantic Tracks are structured transatlantic routes. — [Wikipedia: North_Atlantic_Tracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Tracks)
  > "A structured set of transatlantic flight routes that stretch from eastern North America to western Europe across the Atlantic Ocean"
- They separate aircraft over the ocean where there is little radar coverage. — [Wikipedia: North_Atlantic_Tracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Tracks)
  > "ensure that aircraft are separated over the ocean, where there is little radar coverage"
- Westbound tracks are lettered A, B, C... (A northernmost); eastbound Z, Y, X... — [Wikipedia: North_Atlantic_Tracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Tracks)
  > "Westbound tracks...are indicated by the letters A, B, C, D etc...where A is the northernmost track. Eastbound tracks...are indicated by the letters Z, Y, X, W etc."
- The tracks are created twice daily. — [Wikipedia: North_Atlantic_Tracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Tracks)
  > "created twice daily to take account of the shifting of the winds aloft"
- The routes are used between 29,000 and 41,000 ft. — [Wikipedia: North_Atlantic_Tracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Tracks)
  > "The routes are used for aircraft operating between the altitudes of 29,000 and 41,000 ft (8,800 and 12,500 m) inclusive"
- Jet streams are westerly winds near the tropopause (~30,000 ft). — [Wikipedia: Jet_stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_stream)
  > "westerly winds, flowing west to east around the globe"
- Jet stream winds can reach 400 km/h. — [Wikipedia: Jet_stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_stream)
  > "400 km/h (220 kn; 250 mph)"
- Tracks minimize headwinds and maximize tailwinds. — [Wikipedia: North_Atlantic_Tracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Tracks)
  > "minimize any head winds and maximize tail winds impact on the aircraft"
- Eastbound flights across North America can save about 30 minutes. — [Wikipedia: Jet_stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_stream)
  > "about 30 minutes"
- In 1952 Pan Am cut Tokyo–Honolulu from 18 to 11.5 hours using the jet stream. — [Wikipedia: Jet_stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_stream)
  > "cut the trip time by over one-third, from 18 to 11.5 hours"
- In November 2015 reduced lateral separation halved the track spacing from 60 to 30 nautical miles. — [Wikipedia: North_Atlantic_Tracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Tracks)
  > "In November 2015, a new procedure allowing for reduced lateral separation minima (RLAT) was introduced. RLAT reduces the standard distance between NAT tracks from 60 to 30 nautical miles"
- 500,000 flights went through the system in 2018. — [Wikipedia: North_Atlantic_Tracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Tracks)
  > "500,000 flights went through"

## 9. Why Animals Don't Cross This Invisible Line 🦘🐒🚧 The Wallace Line  (`wallace_line`)
- The line runs through the Lombok Strait between Bali and Lombok, about 35 km. — [Wikipedia: Wallace_Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_Line)
  > "through the Lombok Strait between Bali and Lombok, where the distance is strikingly small, only about 35 kilometers (22 mi)"
- Alfred Russel Wallace identified the boundary in 1859. — [Wikipedia: Wallace_Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_Line)
  > "1859"
- Wallace noticed the division in land mammals and birds. — [Wikipedia: Wallace_Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_Line)
  > "Wallace noticed this clear division in both land mammals and birds during his travels through the East Indies in the 19th century."
- West of the line: Asian fauna such as apes, elephants and monkeys. — [Wikipedia: Wallace_Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_Line)
  > "apes, elephants"
- East of the line: Australasian species such as marsupials. — [Wikipedia: Wallace_Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_Line)
  > "marsupials"
- Exceptions include macaques, pigs and tarsiers on Sulawesi. — [Wikipedia: Wallace_Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_Line)
  > "Exceptions to this include macaques, pigs, and tarsiers on Sulawesi."
- Flora do not follow the Wallace Line to the same extent as fauna. — [Wikipedia: Wallace_Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_Line)
  > "Flora do not follow the Wallace Line to the same extent as fauna, since their colonization events differ in their ability to spread across bodies of water."
- Even when sea level dropped 120 m, the islands never united Asia with Australia. — [Wikipedia: Wallace_Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_Line)
  > "islands became connected, but never uniting Asia with Australia"
- The Sunda Shelf links Borneo, Bali, Java and Sumatra to mainland Asia; the Sahul Shelf connects Australia to New Guinea. — [Wikipedia: Wallace_Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_Line)
  > "The Sunda Shelf links Borneo, Bali, Java, and Sumatra underwater to the mainland of southeastern Asia, while the Sahul Shelf connects Australia to New Guinea and their adjacent islands."
- Deep water between the Sunda and Sahul shelves separated the fauna for over 50 million years. — [Wikipedia: Wallace_Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_Line)

  > "since no regular marine or air traffic routes are within 400 kilometres (250 mi)"
- Sometimes the closest humans are astronauts on the ISS. — [Wikipedia: Pole_of_inaccessibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pole_of_inaccessibility)
  > "sometimes the closest human beings are astronauts aboard the International Space Station when it passes overhead"
- Spacecraft are made to fall there on re-entry. — [Wikipedia: Pole_of_inaccessibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pole_of_inaccessibility)
  > "The wider area is also known as a 'spacecraft cemetery', because hundreds of decommissioned satellites, space stations, and other spacecraft have been made to fall there upon re-entering the atmosphere"
- The area holds the location of the fictional city of R'lyeh in Lovecraft's 1928 story. — [Wikipedia: Point_Nemo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Nemo)
  > "The general area also plays a major role in the 1928 short story 'The Call of Cthulhu' by H. P. Lovecraft, as holding the location of the fictional city of R'lyeh"
- The ISS is planned to crash near Point Nemo in 2031. — [Wikipedia: Point_Nemo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Nemo)
  > "The International Space Station (ISS) is planned to crash into the ocean near Point Nemo in 2031."
